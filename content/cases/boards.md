situation: >-
  Boards is the system of record for corporate governance, used by roughly 70% of the Fortune 1000, by two very different people: administrators and corporate secretaries who live in it daily building books, agendas, and approvals, and directors who open it a handful of times a year for high-stakes moments and have no patience for relearning anything. Diligent charged a premium the product didn’t look like it earned: a navy band header, uppercase section labels, and a ten-item nav where Minutes and Questionnaires were external links. Any change risked breaking directors’ muscle memory and admins’ workflows.
did: >-
  I took direct ownership: ran the client interviews, owned the information architecture, and chose the visual direction. I made two contrarian calls. I shipped the visual redesign first and held the IA work about 90 days, because a visual change is low-risk and reversible and buys trust, while an IA change is where muscle memory breaks. Shipping the skin first earned permission to move the furniture. And I modernized the legacy product clients actually used, not the next-gen one, so the modernized old product leapfrogged the new. Then I moved the IA from where things are stored to what needs you. [DECIDE: what has changed in my scope since the original draft, such as team growth, rollout ownership, additional surfaces]
outcomes:
  - "Books went from where things are stored to what needs you. Current and Archived Books collapse into one searchable Books list, and News & Analytics stops being a destination and becomes curated updates on Home. Where the old Home said “There are no actions at the moment,” the new one leads with “Waiting for your review,” and directors and administrators get explicitly separate views."
  - "The reader has structured agenda navigation and relevance-ordered search with inline highlights, built for the job directors open Boards to do."
  - "Approvals tell “16 of 16 responded” apart from “2 of 4 independent directors,” which is governance nuance, not a generic workflow."
  - "The foundation made room for AI. GovernAI sits in the reader and answers with cited sources, including drafting briefing notes."
  - "Usability research shows stronger findability for core content, and Forward Planner usage increased roughly 30× after being surfaced through the new experience. That is evidence of improved orientation and feature discovery, not yet proof of higher task completion, satisfaction, or retention."
