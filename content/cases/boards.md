situation: \>-
  Boards is the system of record for corporate governance, used by roughly 70% of the Fortune 1000. Diligent charged a premium the product didn't look like it earned, and any change risked disrupting power users who open it a few times a year.
did: \>-
  I took direct ownership: ran the client interviews, owned the information architecture, and chose the visual direction. I made two contrarian calls. I shipped the visual redesign first and held the IA work \~90 days. And I built for the legacy product clients actually used, not the next-gen one, so the modernized old product leapfrogged the new.
outcomes:
  - "The redesigned Boards UI is directionally better because it replaces a confusing, workflow-misaligned information architecture with a clearer, task-oriented structure. Usability research shows stronger findability for core content, and Forward Planner usage increased roughly 30× after being surfaced through the new experience. This is evidence of improved orientation and feature discovery—not yet as proof of higher task completion, satisfaction, or retention."
asking: A better destination doesn't make change easy. How do you get teams to ship early and imperfect when it's never been the norm?
