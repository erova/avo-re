### Why this experiment exists

In complex enterprise products, users often *already have* the tools they need — they just don’t always know the best or safest way to use them.

This experiment explores a simple question:

> What if the system could notice when a user is “done” with something, and help with what usually comes next — without popups, tours, or nagging?

The focus here is **Books**: a surface where admins frequently archive, export, or share materials after a meeting concludes.

---

### The idea

Instead of treating actions like **Archive**, **Export to PDF**, or **Send to Data Room** as isolated commands, this experiment treats them as signals:

- Archiving usually means the meeting is finished
- Exporting usually means materials will be shared externally
- Sending to the Data Room usually implies an audit or record-keeping need

The system can respond in two modes:

- **Coaching** — suggesting better or more complete follow-up actions
- **Agentic** — performing those follow-ups automatically, with clear receipts and undo controls

---

### What this prototype illustrates

- Ambient, contextual messaging (not global notifications)
- A clear difference between *suggesting* and *acting*
- Guardrails that preserve user trust (review, undo, cautious modes)
- A system that can be a little proud of itself when it saves time

This is intentionally **not** a production-ready UX — it’s a way to reason about timing, tone, and trust.
