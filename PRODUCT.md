# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Chris Avore's portfolio at avo.re. Three readers, in order of how fast they leave: executive recruiters (30–60 seconds, need scope, level, companies, a few numbers, a résumé link), product and engineering executives deciding whether to hire a design leader (3–5 minutes, want judgment and business outcomes), and senior design leaders who will read everything and want the honest version, including what was hard and what is still unresolved.

## Product Purpose

A single scrolling home page that presents Chris as a design leader for high-stakes, regulated products who also builds in code and has a point of view on AI and org design. It links out to case studies, essays, and prototypes. Success: a recruiter gets the picture in a skim, and a peer finds enough depth and candor to remember it.

## Brand Personality

Sophisticated, mature, refined. Confident and alive, never loud. Fluent in advanced front-end craft, expressed as subtle cues (depth, layering, motion that responds) and not as spectacle. Editorial in feel: closer to a serious long-form magazine than to a designer's template.

## Anti-references

- pudding.cool: playful, illustrated, sticker-like, loud saturated tiles. Too fun and too loud for this audience.
- chelseawatson.com: the designer-portfolio template. A headline of self-descriptors, a grid of trait blurbs under small tracked all-caps labels, a client logo strip, enthusiasm-as-copy. Too boilerplate.
- Static, 2002-style pages with no sense of life.
- The "board book" concept from the original brief, and the brief's visual rules (palette, type, don't list, "only the state viewer animates"). Rejected.

Positive references (editorial, long-form): Asterisk magazine, Works in Progress, Quanta Magazine. Admired: large expressive serif type, readable column, imagery that runs wider than the text and layers, floating panels over photos, a margin rail of scroll cues.

## Design Principles

1. **Editorial, not template.** Confident typography and a real reading column. Structure comes from layout and type, not from labeled boxes, trait grids, or logo strips.
2. **Depth carries the personality.** Imagery layers, crops, and runs past the column; panels float over it. Motion shows the page is alive and shows craft, and each movement has a job.
3. **Color is committed, not decorative.** Large color fields, with pink and green as the working pair. Deep and considered, not candy.
4. **Honesty over polish.** Say what was hard and what is unresolved. The design should feel as candid as the copy.
5. **Skim first, reward the reader who stays.** Every section works in a glance and has more underneath.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA contrast, visible focus, keyboard-accessible interactives. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns motion off or reduces it to a plain fade. `prefers-color-scheme` dark mode. Color is never the only carrier of meaning (pink and green must also differ in lightness). Lighthouse 95+ and no layout shift: animate transform and opacity only.
